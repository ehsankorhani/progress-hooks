When a giant star runs out of fuel, its core can collapse into a **neutron star** —
a city-sized ball roughly 20 km across that packs in more mass than our Sun.

A sugar-cube of neutron star material would weigh about **a billion tons** here on Earth —
roughly the mass of Mount Everest squeezed into your hand.

And some of them spin. A lot. The fastest known pulsars rotate more than
**700 times every second**, spinning so fast that their equators move at a
noticeable fraction of the speed of light.

As they spin, they sweep beams of radio energy across the galaxy like lighthouses —
which is exactly how we found them.
