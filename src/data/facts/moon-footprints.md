There is no wind, no rain, and no flowing water on the Moon, so there is almost
nothing to erase the marks left on its surface.

The footprints pressed into the fine lunar dust by the Apollo astronauts in the 1960s
and 70s are still there today, perfectly crisp. They will remain until the slow,
endless rain of micrometeorites eventually grinds them away — a process that takes
**millions of years**.

Every human institution you can think of — every country, company, and language —
has existed for a blink by comparison. The footprints will almost certainly outlast us all.
