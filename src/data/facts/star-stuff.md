The hydrogen in your body is as old as the universe itself — forged in the Big Bang roughly
13.8 billion years ago. But almost everything else has a more dramatic origin story.

The **calcium** in your bones, the **iron** in your blood, and the **oxygen** you are
breathing right now were all assembled deep inside the cores of stars that lived and died
before the Sun was even born. When those stars ran out of fuel, they exploded as
supernovae, scattering their elements across space.

Billions of years later, that debris drifted into the cloud of gas and dust that collapsed
to form our solar system — and eventually became you.

> "We are made of star-stuff." — Carl Sagan

As Carl Sagan liked to say: *we are a way for the cosmos to know itself.*
