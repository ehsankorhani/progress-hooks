Venus is the slowest-spinning planet in the solar system. One full rotation takes about
**243 Earth days**, while a trip around the Sun takes only about **225 Earth days**.
So technically, a Venusian day is longer than a Venusian year.

Stranger still, Venus spins **backwards** compared to almost every other planet. On its
surface, the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east — if you could ever see it
through the thick clouds of sulfuric acid.

Because day and night are both measured relative to the moving Sun, a full solar day on
Venus actually lasts about **117 Earth days**. Confusing? Absolutely. Wonderful? Also yes.
