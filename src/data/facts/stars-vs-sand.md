Astronomers estimate the observable universe contains somewhere around
**10²² to 10²⁴ stars** — that is a 1 followed by up to 24 zeros.

A 2016 study estimated there are about **7.5 × 10¹⁸ grains of sand** on all of
Earth's beaches. Even against the conservative star count, the universe wins by
a landslide — and sand on beaches does not include deserts, which barely helps
the sand's case.

Every one of those stars is a sun. Many have planets. The scale is genuinely
beyond human intuition, and that is half the fun of astronomy.
