Saturn is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium, and its average density
is only about **0.687 g/cm³** — roughly two-thirds the density of water.

That makes Saturn the only planet in the solar system that is less dense than water.
In theory, if you could build a bathtub big enough, Saturn would float in it.

In practice you would need a tub about 120,000 km wide, and the physics of getting
a planet to stay nicely afloat are… complicated. But on paper: Saturn floats. 🛁
